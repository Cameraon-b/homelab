# AETHER Log #41 – Knowledge, Monitoring, and Lifecycle Management

**Date:** August 11, 2026

The previous phase marked AETHER's transition from building infrastructure to operating it.

GLPI had been deployed as the new IT service management platform, asset inventory had begun, and tickets were being used to track work that previously existed in memory, scattered notes, or documentation.

That transition continued during this phase.

Rather than deploying another major piece of infrastructure, the focus shifted toward maintaining the environment that already exists: protecting documentation, improving the knowledge architecture, expanding monitoring, tracking real incidents, and planning for the eventual replacement of aging systems.

AETHER is beginning to develop a lifecycle.

---

## Protecting the Knowledge Base

BookStack has become one of the most important services in AETHER.

What began as a place to record configuration notes has grown into a substantial operational knowledge base containing hundreds of pages covering infrastructure, networking, services, troubleshooting, recovery procedures, projects, and the history of the environment.

Before making major changes to that knowledge base, a dedicated backup was created.

The external AETHER backup drive was mounted on Nora and a pre-change snapshot was created containing:

- A complete BookStack database dump.
- An archive of the BookStack application data.
- The Docker Compose configuration.
- Exact container image information for BookStack and MariaDB.
- SHA-256 checksums for backup validation.

The archive was inspected, the database dump was verified to have completed successfully, and all recorded checksums passed validation.

BookStack was then restarted and tested successfully.

This created a known rollback point before allowing any significant restructuring of AETHER's documentation.

The exercise reinforced an increasingly important rule:

> A backup is considerably more useful when its contents and integrity have actually been verified.

---

## The Documentation Problem Changes

AETHER no longer has a problem with insufficient documentation.

It has almost the opposite problem.

Documentation was created continuously as the environment grew, and different organizational patterns developed during different phases of the project.

Information about a single system could exist in several locations. Some subjects had duplicate pages. Operational information could be mixed with learning material. Older architecture remained useful historically but sometimes existed beside newer information without an obvious distinction.

The information existed.

Finding the correct information was becoming harder.

BookStack had therefore reached the point where its architecture needed to evolve alongside the infrastructure it describes.

---

## Mira and the BookStack Restructuring

Mira was given one of the largest individual tasks yet delegated to an AETHER agent.

Her first assignment was to analyze and improve BookStack while preserving all existing knowledge.

The governing requirement was simple:

> **Do not delete information.**

Mira could add context, improve pages, consolidate information, create navigation, and identify outdated material, but historical information was to remain preserved.

Her initial pass improved the operational front door and added or refreshed documentation for several current systems and services.

However, the deeper problem remained.

The organization itself needed to change.

Mira was therefore asked to perform a second analysis without making changes and propose an entirely new BookStack architecture.

The resulting proposal separated documentation according to its purpose, including areas for:

- Current inventory.
- Services.
- Operations and runbooks.
- Architecture.
- Projects and development.
- Learning and reference material.
- Historical and legacy information.

A particularly useful distinction emerged from the proposal:

> **What exists belongs in inventory.  
> Why it was designed that way belongs in architecture.  
> How to operate or repair it belongs in operations.  
> How the underlying technology works belongs in reference material.**

After reviewing the proposal, Mira was authorized to begin the restructuring.

The goal is not to make BookStack smaller.

The goal is to make hundreds of pages of accumulated knowledge predictable and navigable without destroying the history that produced them.

---

## Bringing BookStack to GitHub

The BookStack restructuring also creates an opportunity to complete a project that has been planned for several months.

The public AETHER repository currently contains extensive chronological logs showing how the environment developed.

Those logs demonstrate the process:

- What was built.
- What broke.
- How problems were investigated.
- What architectural decisions were made.
- What was learned.

BookStack represents the other side of that work.

It contains the resulting operational knowledge.

Once Mira completes the internal restructuring, she will create a separate sanitized Markdown representation of selected BookStack documentation inside her workspace.

She will not directly modify GitHub or Gitea.

The generated files will be manually reviewed before being added to the public repository.

This maintains an intentional trust boundary while finally allowing the repository to demonstrate the documentation work that has existed behind AETHER throughout its development.

The relationship between the two forms of documentation can be summarized simply:

> **Logs show the journey. Documentation shows the resulting system.**

Cass serves as the primary host for Hermes and also supports local LLM experimentation.

Hermes functions primarily as an engineering assistant focused on development, technical analysis, design, troubleshooting, and infrastructure planning.

## A Real Incident Appears

Shortly after expanding the operational workflow, Uptime Kuma reported that WIN-CLIENT01 was unavailable.

Investigation revealed that the Windows evaluation license had finally expired after its 90-day evaluation period.

Previously, this would likely have become another mental note:

> I need to fix that eventually.

Instead, an incident was created in GLPI.

The problem is known, its impact is low, and immediate restoration is unnecessary.

That means it does not need to become an emergency.

This produced one of the first naturally occurring examples of the monitoring and ticketing systems working together:

> **Monitoring detected the failure.  
> Investigation identified the cause.  
> GLPI recorded the work.**

The issue can now remain unresolved without being forgotten.

---

## Planning the Future of WIN-CLIENT01

The expiration also created an architectural question.

WIN-CLIENT01 could eventually become a permanently licensed Windows domain client, providing a persistent environment for Active Directory, Group Policy, DNS, authentication, software deployment, and Windows administration.

Alternatively, its temporary nature could become intentional.

Rebuilding the client periodically would provide repeated practice with the complete workstation lifecycle:

- Provisioning a virtual machine.
- Installing Windows.
- Configuring networking.
- Joining the domain.
- Assigning the correct OU.
- Applying and validating Group Policy.
- Installing required software.
- Registering and updating the asset in GLPI.
- Restoring monitoring.
- Validating authentication and DNS.
- Retiring the previous installation.

Rather than treating evaluation expiration as something to work around, the VM could become an intentionally replaceable enterprise workstation.

The final approach has not yet been selected.

The goal is to find a maintainable solution while using the problem as an opportunity to practice real endpoint lifecycle management.

---

## Planning Ahead for WIN-DC01

WIN-DC01 presents a much more important version of the same problem.

The Windows Server evaluation still has approximately 110 days remaining, and the domain controller is currently operating normally.

Unlike WIN-CLIENT01, however, WIN-DC01 provides critical infrastructure for AETHER.

It currently supports:

- Active Directory Domain Services.
- DNS.
- Authentication.
- Kerberos.
- Group Policy.
- Domain identity.

The objective is therefore to address its lifecycle before expiration becomes an incident.

Rather than cloning the existing virtual machine or attempting to preserve the current installation indefinitely, the preferred approach is to eventually build a replacement domain controller.

A future WIN-DC02 would be introduced into the existing domain alongside WIN-DC01.

The migration would provide practical experience with:

- Deploying a new Windows Server.
- Joining an existing Active Directory domain.
- Promoting an additional domain controller.
- AD and DNS replication.
- SYSVOL replication.
- Operating multiple domain controllers.
- Validating Windows and Linux authentication.
- Validating Group Policy and DNS.
- Transferring FSMO roles.
- Demoting the previous domain controller.
- Retiring an infrastructure asset cleanly.

WIN-DC01 would remain operational during the migration and would only be retired after WIN-DC02 had been fully validated.

This turns an upcoming licensing constraint into a realistic infrastructure migration project.

More importantly, it demonstrates the difference between reacting to failure and planning around a known lifecycle event.

WIN-CLIENT01 became an incident.

WIN-DC01 can become a planned change.

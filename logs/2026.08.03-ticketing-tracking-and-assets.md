# AETHER Log #40 – From Infrastructure to Operations

**Date:** August 3, 2026

The previous phase of AETHER focused on building infrastructure.

Active Directory had become operational. Linux systems were joined to the domain. Pan replaced the previous standalone Kali installation as a virtual machine. Iris became the WireGuard and FreedomBox gateway into the private network. Documentation matured through BookStack, monitoring through Uptime Kuma, and remote administration through WireGuard and SSH.

Those systems were no longer experiments.

They had become part of the operational environment.

The next objective was not adding another server.

The next objective was learning how to operate the environment that already existed.

---

## Choosing an Operational Platform

The original plan was to deploy Peppermint as AETHER's ticketing system.

Before installation, the project itself was evaluated.

During that review it became clear that Peppermint had recently been archived and was no longer receiving active development.

Rather than deploying software that had already reached end-of-life, the decision was made to adopt GLPI instead.

The change reflected a broader architectural principle that will guide future decisions:

> Infrastructure should be built upon actively maintained software whenever practical.

Changing direction before deployment was considerably easier than replacing the platform later after operational data had accumulated.

---

## Troubleshooting Before Deployment

Deployment did not succeed immediately.

The official Docker Compose stack launched successfully, but the database container repeatedly failed during startup.

Instead of modifying random configuration values, the logs became the primary source of information.

The failure ultimately traced back to an architectural constraint rather than a Docker configuration problem.

The MySQL container required an x86-64-v2 processor baseline that Nora's AMD Athlon II processor does not support.

The problem was not Docker.

The problem was not GLPI.

The problem was a mismatch between software requirements and available hardware.

Rather than forcing newer software onto older hardware, MariaDB 10.11 LTS was selected as the database platform, restoring compatibility while remaining on a fully supported database.

The deployment also reinforced another operational lesson.

Configuration validation should always occur before services are started.

The Docker Compose configuration was validated, YAML syntax corrected, credentials regenerated after accidental exposure, and only then was the stack deployed successfully.

---

## Expanding the AETHER Portal

With GLPI operational, the Homepage dashboard was reorganized to better reflect the architecture of AETHER.

Instead of functioning as a simple collection of bookmarks, the dashboard now organizes services into functional areas:

- Infrastructure
- Operations
- Knowledge
- Development

Additional systems including GLPI, Iris, Pan, and RustDesk were incorporated into the environment.

Rather than listing applications, the Homepage increasingly represents the structure of the environment itself.

---

## Introducing Operational Workflows

Deploying GLPI immediately changed how future work will be managed.

Previously, ideas often existed in memory, scattered notes, or BookStack pages.

Now infrastructure work can be tracked through formal operational workflows.

Changes such as deploying services, updating documentation, modifying networking, or improving security can exist as tracked work with priorities, status, history, and completion records.

BookStack documents **how** systems are built.

GLPI records **what work** is being performed on those systems.

Uptime Kuma reports **whether those systems remain healthy.**

Each platform now serves a distinct operational role within AETHER.

---

## Building an Asset Inventory

Following the deployment of GLPI, work began on building a complete inventory of AETHER.

The goal extends well beyond tracking servers.

Physical computers, virtual machines, networking equipment, monitors, peripherals, and infrastructure services will all become managed assets within the environment.

As AETHER continues to grow, it is becoming increasingly unrealistic to remember every dependency, hostname, and future project from memory alone.

GLPI becomes the authoritative answer to a simple question:

> **What exists within AETHER?**

BookStack explains how those assets work.

GLPI records that they exist.

Together they begin forming a complete operational picture of the environment.

---

## Looking Forward

The deployment of GLPI marks the beginning of a new stage for AETHER.

Previous phases focused primarily on building infrastructure.

The next phase focuses on operating that infrastructure.

Future work will increasingly revolve around:

- Maintaining a complete asset inventory.
- Tracking infrastructure changes through tickets.
- Recording incidents and resolutions.
- Improving operational documentation.
- Continuing development of Nexus as the coordination and audit layer across the environment.

Rather than acting as isolated systems, BookStack, GLPI, Uptime Kuma, Homepage, Active Directory, and eventually Nexus are beginning to function together as parts of a single operational platform.

---

## Final State

At the end of the session:

- GLPI has replaced Peppermint as AETHER's IT Service Management platform.
- Deployment issues were resolved through architectural troubleshooting rather than trial and error.
- MariaDB was adopted to maintain compatibility with Nora's hardware.
- The Homepage dashboard now better reflects the overall architecture of AETHER.
- Initial operational tickets have been created to track ongoing infrastructure work.
- Asset inventory has begun, with physical systems, virtual machines, networking equipment, and other infrastructure being entered into GLPI.
- AETHER has begun transitioning from simply building infrastructure to operating and managing a production-like environment.

The defining accomplishment of this session was not deploying another Docker container.

It was establishing the operational foundation that will allow AETHER to continue growing without relying solely on memory.

The environment is no longer simply a collection of services.

It is becoming an infrastructure that can document itself, inventory itself, monitor itself, and record its own history.

The laboratory is becoming an operations center.
